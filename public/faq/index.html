Frequently asked questions

What the indices are, and what they are not.

The questions below are the ones the Institute is actually asked, grouped by the categories that come up most often.

What the Institute measures

What do the indices actually measure?

Attention. Each index measures how strongly a state is turned toward Christian education in a given month, against the other forty-nine states in that same month. A state near the top is one where attention to Christian schooling runs high for its month; a state near the bottom is one where it runs low. A reading places a state against the others; it does not count anything.

Why five indices instead of one?

The Master Index broadly measures attention to Christian education in a state. The four educational level indices measure it at the Preschool, Elementary School, Middle, and High School levels, because a family’s focus shifts as a child moves through school, and a single figure conceals that.

Is the Master Index the total of the other four?

No. The five are computed from disjoint constituent sets. The Master Index is its own measure, and adding the five together produces a number that means nothing.

What does a reading of 130 mean?

On its own, very little, and the Institute does not publish index levels for that reason. Each constituent is rescaled so that the constituent’s own median state reads 100 in that month, and the published index is built from a set of those constituents, so the index itself does not sit at 100 in the middle. What is published is the position among the states measured that month, and the ratio to the median state in the same month: a state at 1.30 times the median sits 30% above the middle of the country. It does not mean 30% more of anything.

Can I compare a reading in 2008 with a reading in 2026?

You can compare positions and you can compare ratios to the median. You cannot treat the difference between two readings as a quantity, because a reading is not a quantity in either window.

Data and sources

Where do the school figures come from?

The National Center for Education Statistics Private School Universe Survey. Schools and enrollments are computed from the school-level records, weighted by the survey’s own final weight.

Where does the population denominator come from?

The Census Bureau’s Population Estimates Program, resident population aged five through seventeen, at the reference date of 1 July of the vintage year.

Why ages five through seventeen?

That range matches what the school census counts: Kindergarten through Grade 12 and ungraded, excluding prekindergarten. A denominator that included four-year-olds would count children the numerator cannot contain, and would understate every rate.

Which population vintage does a given report use?

The latest vintage that was public on the day that report would have been published. A report published in February 2024 divides by Vintage 2022, because Vintage 2023 did not exist until 27 June 2024.

Will a published figure ever change?

Not silently. Every source extract is stored with its checksum, so a figure cannot move underneath itself. Where a correction is required, it is published as a correction with its date.

Can I get the underlying data?

Yes. Every figure on this site is published as a data file alongside it, in JSON for programs, and in CSV for spreadsheets, under the same license as everything else here.

Which schools are counted

How is a Christian school defined?

By the religious orientation the federal census records for it. Orientation codes one through sixteen and twenty through twenty-eight are Christian and are included.

Which schools are excluded?

Islamic, Jewish, and Latter-day Saints orientations, and nonsectarian schools. The residual “other” category is adjudicated school by school against a five-test rule, and the adjudication is published.

What happens when the census contradicts itself?

Where the religious flag and the orientation code disagree, the orientation code governs. The flag says whether a school is religious at all; the code says which religion, which is the question that decides the universe.

Are Catholic schools included?

Yes. Catholic schools are Christian schools, and excluding them would misdescribe Christian schooling in most of the country.

Christian Education Market Areas

What is a Christian Education Market Area?

A group of whole counties anchored on a population center, within which a family can realistically choose a school. There are 262 of them.

Why not use states, or counties?

A state is too coarse to describe a schooling decision, and a county is too fine to contain one. A Christian school student is not transported hundreds of miles to attend school.

Why whole counties?

So that every federal count published on county geography sums into a market area without estimation. A split county would require an assumption in every figure.

What happened to Connecticut?

Connecticut replaced its eight counties with nine planning regions. The Institute’s five Connecticut market areas are defined on the planning regions, and each population vintage carries its own mapping.

Does Christian school enrollments per 1,000 measure how many places are available?

No. It counts children enrolled. The federal census records enrollment and does not ask a school its capacity, so no source exists from which an availability figure could be built. A high figure means a large share of a place’s children are in Christian schools; it does not mean there is room for more. The metric was published as “seats per 1,000” until 31 August 2026, when it was renamed because the word invited the opposite reading.

Why do the market areas not add up to the states?

Of the 262 market areas, 261 are anchored in the fifty states and one is the District of Columbia, which is not a state and is not in any Spring Vale Christian Education Institute Region. School counts are also weighted estimates rounded at each level of aggregation. A state can therefore differ by a school or two from the sum of the market areas inside it.

Are these the same as Designated Market Areas?

No. A Designated Market Area is a television market. A Christian Education Market Area is defined by the Institute for Christian schooling, and the two are not interchangeable.

Using and citing the work

What license applies?

Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. You may copy, redistribute, and build on anything here for any purpose, including commercially, with attribution.

How should I cite a figure?

Credit the Institute, name what you are citing, link to it, and cite the month with the data. A figure reproduced without the month it was measured in is not our figure.

May I write that an index “grew”?

No. A movement in an index is a change in standing rather than growth or decline in any quantity. Rank, rank movement, and the ratio to the median state are the forms.

What if someone misrepresents the indices?

Adapted material must be identified as adapted. Where an adaptation misstates what the indices measure, the Institute will exercise its right under section 3(a)(3) of the license to require that attribution to the Institute be removed from it.

Publication and corrections

When does the monthly release publish?

The second Tuesday of the month after the one it covers. That day belongs permanently to the monthly release.

When do the annual volumes publish?

In January, against the completed reporting year, which ends on 31 December.

How are errors handled?

A correction is published with its date and what it changed. Every document is rebuilt from current data before release, so a correction reaches every place the figure appears rather than only the one that was reported.

Who funds the Institute?

The Institute is an initiative of Spring Vale Christian School and is funded by the school and by donors. It accepts no funding conditioned on a finding.
