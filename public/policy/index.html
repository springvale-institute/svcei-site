Policy Monitor

The qualitative record beside the quantitative one.

The indices record what happened. The Policy Monitor records what was decided: the issues, decision points, statutes, rulings, and administrative changes that bear on Christian schooling in each state.

What it is

A dated record, kept separately on purpose.

The Monitor is a chronology of policy events by state and by date, each entered with its source document. It is kept apart from the indices for one reason: an index reading and a policy event are two different kinds of evidence, and joining them in a sentence asserts a cause the Institute has not measured.

Where a report shows a sharp movement in the series alongside a policy event in the same month, both are shown, both are dated, and the report states that the Institute does not attribute a cause.

Christian Education Latitude

How much room public policy leaves a Christian school.

The Institute measures two things today. Attention is how strongly a place is oriented toward Christian schooling. Schools is how much Christian schooling exists there. Neither says anything about the terms on which a Christian school is allowed to operate, and those terms are set by statute, rule, and ruling. Latitude is that third thing.

It is a description of a public policy environment. It is not a forecast, it is not an endorsement, and it is not a claim that latitude produces schools or attention. Where Latitude and Schools disagree is interesting precisely because neither is derived from the other.

Five components, each scored 0 to 4 against anchors written so that a coder is never asked whether a state is friendly, only whether an instrument says a particular thing. Every score cites the instrument it was taken from and carries the evidence tier of that instrument. A component with no instrument is left uncoded rather than guessed.

Where the record is watching

How recently a policy event was recorded in each state.

A monitor is only as good as its reach, and a reader is entitled to see the reach rather than take it on trust. States are shaded by how recently the Institute recorded a dated event there. A state left white has no event in the record at all, which means the Institute has not yet read one into it. It does not mean nothing happened there.

Why the first layer is coverage rather than a score

An index published before its inventory is finished has one obligation, which is to show a reader how much of it is finished, and to show that first. The default layer of this map is the count of components coded in each jurisdiction. A jurisdiction that has not been coded is drawn as uncoded rather than shaded a middling color that would read as a finding.

Only a jurisdiction coded on all five components takes a rank. The first build of this index ranked every jurisdiction that carried any score, and it put eighteen states scored on one component above a state scored on five, because the mean of a single four is four. A mean over a partial component set is not comparable to a mean over a full one, and publishing the two in one column would have ranked how little was known about each state.

How a qualitative index is kept from becoming an opinion

Attention and Schools are arithmetic on files. Latitude is judgment applied to documents, and judgment is where an index quietly becomes an opinion. Four rules hold it, and all four are borrowed from the political science indices that have survived scrutiny rather than invented here.

Anchored ordinals. Every level of every component is anchored to an observable fact about an instrument, never to an adjective.

Every score cites its instrument. A score with no instrument attached is not published. This is the rule that makes the index checkable and the one that costs the most to keep.

Double coding, with the agreement published. Two coders score each jurisdiction independently, a third adjudicates, and the rate of first-pass agreement is published for each component. An index that does not publish its inter-coder agreement is asking to be trusted rather than read.

The evidence tier travels with the score. A component scored from a statute and one scored from an association’s summary are not the same claim, and the map shows which is which rather than averaging them into a single confident number. The tier filter lets a reader see the index built from official sources alone.

What the machine sweep has found, and not yet read

A weekly sweep reads published court opinions from the federal courts and every state appellate court, matches them against the five instrument classes, and writes candidates into a queue. It writes candidates and never entries. Nothing it finds affects a Latitude score or appears on the Chronicle until a person reads the opinion and promotes it.

That split is the whole design of the sweep. Finding is mechanical and tedious and a machine is better at it. Judging is where the errors live: a case on private school accreditation and a case on private prison accreditation match the same words, and an opinion that never mentions religion can matter more to a Christian school than one that does.

How it is published

As a rank among the fifty-one jurisdictions and as a ratio to the median jurisdiction, never as a level. A composite of ordinals carries no unit, and a reader handed a number like 63.4 will treat it as a quantity of something. The five components are equally weighted, because unequal weights would be a claim about which freedom matters most, and that is a judgment the Institute has no standing to make. The component scores are published beside the composite so that a reader who disagrees can reweight them.

Correct use

Everything the Institute publishes is released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. You may copy it, redistribute it, and build on it for any purpose, including commercially, provided you meet the four conditions below.

Attribute
Credit the Spring Vale Christian Education Institute, name what you are citing, and link to it.
Cite the month with the data
Every index value belongs to the month it was measured in. A figure reproduced without its month is not our figure.
Never turn a position into a count
The indices measure relative position: where a state stands against the other forty-nine, and against its own record. They are not counts of children, schools, or enrollments, and a movement in an index is a change in standing rather than growth or decline in any quantity.
Mark what you changed
Adapted material must be identified as adapted, under section 3(a)(1)(B) of the license. Where an adaptation misstates what the indices measure, or presents altered figures as the Institute’s own, the Institute will exercise its right under section 3(a)(3) to require that attribution to the Institute be removed from it.
