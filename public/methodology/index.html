Methodology

How the Institute measures, and what it will not claim.

This page is the complete public account of the measurement. It is written so that a reader who disagrees with a conclusion can find the step they disagree with.

The measurement

Two things are measured, and they are never mixed.

The Institute publishes two families of figure. They answer different questions, they come from different places, and they never share an axis or a sentence.

Attention

The five indices measure engagement with Christian education: how much of a state’s attention is directed at Christian schooling in a given month, relative to the other states in the same month. This is the Institute’s own measurement. The constituent list is locked across all 271 months, so a movement in the series is a movement in the country rather than a change in what was counted.

Each constituent is rescaled by the median across the fifty states, so that each constituent has a median of 100. That is what makes a reading comparable across a twenty-two-year series: the scale is re-anchored every month, and no reading carries a unit. It does not follow that the published index reads 100 at the middle of the country, and the section below shows what it does read.

Schools

Schools is a count. It comes from the federal private school census and is reported as schools, enrollments, and Christian school enrollments per 1,000 children. It is the answer to “what exists”, where an index answers “where does this state stand”.

What the rate counts, and what it does not. It counts children enrolled. The federal census records enrollment; it does not ask a school its capacity and does not record unfilled places, so no source exists from which an availability figure could honestly be built. The rate therefore measures provision taken up rather than provision available. A market area with a high figure has a large share of its children in Christian schools; it does not follow that it has room for more, and the Institute does not claim that it does. The metric was formerly published as “seats per 1,000”, which invited exactly the opposite reading and was renamed on 31 August 2026.

A provision figure and an index reading are never combined into a single number, never plotted on a shared axis, and never described as though one caused the other. Where the two are shown together, they are shown as two quantities with two labels.

What the median state actually reads

The most misunderstood number in the series, drawn rather than asserted.

Every index built this way invites one assumption: that the middle of the country reads 100. It does not, and the reason is arithmetic rather than judgment. Each constituent is rescaled to a median of 100, the published index combines constituents, and the median of a combination is not the combination of the medians. A state at the middle of the index is not at the middle of every constituent that built it.

So rather than ask a reader to take that on trust, here is the figure itself: the median state’s reading, every month, on whichever index you choose. The dashed line is 100.

The shape of that line is not a finding. It is a property of the construction, and it is the clearest possible illustration of why a relative measure carries no growth claim: the level of the median carries no unit, is not comparable from one month to the next, and a rise in it measures nothing about the country. Nothing the Institute publishes describes growth or decline in any quantity, and this chart is not one.

What it does settle is how to read a single figure. A state at 50 is not “half the median” unless the median that month happened to be 100, which it never was. Where one state is being described, the Institute uses the ratio to the median state instead, because a ratio needs no median stated and is comparable across the whole record.

How many constituents carried signal

Published as its own count, because a reader is entitled to it.

Each index is built from a fixed set of measured quantities, which the Institute calls constituents. A constituent has to register in enough of the fifty states to be placed on a common scale with the others. One that registers in too few is set to zero in every state, so it adds nothing anywhere. Every state is lowered by the same amount, which means no state moves in rank and no state moves relative to the median state. The count below reports, for each index, how many of its constituents carried a reading.

The count is a tally of constituents. It is not an index, it is not a reading, and it is never drawn on an index axis. One month’s count is not compared with another’s as though the country had changed: a difference in the count is a difference in what there was to measure, not a difference in Christian education.

The universe

Every Christian school, and only Christian schools.

The school census is taken by the National Center for Education Statistics Private School Universe Survey. A school enters the universe on its religious orientation as the survey records it, not on its own description of itself and not on the broader religious flag.

Orientation codes one through sixteen and twenty through twenty-eight are Christian and are included. Islamic, Jewish, and Latter-day Saints orientations are excluded, as are nonsectarian schools. The residual “other” category is adjudicated school by school against a five-test rule, and the adjudication is published.

Where the survey’s religious flag and its orientation code disagree, the orientation code governs. The flag answers whether a school is religious at all; the orientation code answers which religion, which is the question that decides the universe.

School names are printed as the school writes them rather than as the census stores them. The census file records names in upper case with punctuation removed, which turns Saint Mary’s into a name no school uses, and no school name is ever truncated in anything the Institute publishes.

Sources: National Center for Education Statistics, Private School Universe Survey, 2021–22, public-use data file; and Private School Survey: Public-Use Data User’s Manual for 2021–22 School Year, NCES 2024-011.

Christian Education Market Areas

Whole counties, anchored on a population center.

A Christian Education Market Area is a group of whole counties anchored on a population center, within which a family can realistically choose a school. It is the geography the Institute counts provision on, because a state is too coarse to describe a decision and a county is too fine to contain one.

A Christian Education Market Area is a group of whole, contiguous counties that never crosses a state line, anchored on a population center, and holding at least 30,000 school-age children in total. Anchors are placed within sixty miles of the counties they draw; where a remaining area falls below the threshold it is merged into its nearest neighbor, so a small number of areas — chiefly in Alaska and the Mountain West — span considerably more than sixty miles.

Market areas are built from whole counties so that every federal count published on county geography can be summed into them without estimation. There are 262 of them, they are named, and each page lists its counties by name.

Two geographies changed underneath us

Connecticut replaced its eight counties with nine planning regions, and the Census county files carry the old codes through Vintage 2021 and the new ones from Vintage 2022. The Institute’s five Connecticut market areas are defined on the planning regions, so each vintage carries its own county-to-market mapping. Alaska split Valdez-Cordova into Chugach and Copper River, and both successors are carried.

Neither correction is asserted. Each is proved: with them applied, county populations reproduce the published market populations exactly, and every market area inside a state sums to that state’s own figure.

Coverage

Two measures, two universes, stated plainly.

The Institute measures two things and they do not cover the same ground. Saying so once here is better than a footnote wherever a total appears.

Attention: fifty states

The five indices are computed for the fifty states. The District of Columbia is not measured and no reading is estimated for it. Within a given month an index may be measurable on fewer than fifty, where there is not enough to place a state against the others; every report states how many states its index was measured on, and that figure is never assumed.

Schools: fifty-one jurisdictions

The school census covers the fifty states and the District of Columbia. Those fifty-one jurisdictions are organized into 262 Christian Education Market Areas: 261 anchored in the fifty states, and one in the District of Columbia.

What follows from that

The eight Spring Vale Christian Education Institute Regions are groupings of states, so they contain 261 market areas between them and the District of Columbia belongs to none of them. No state total and no Region total sums to the national figure, and that is the reason.

School and enrollment counts are weighted estimates. They are rounded at each level of aggregation. A state total can therefore differ by a school or two from the sum of the market areas inside it, and across the country the fifty state totals and the 262 market totals differ by three schools. Each figure reconciles to its own source. Population figures carry no such rounding and reconcile exactly.

Vintage

A report uses the data that were public on the day it would have published.

This is the rule that governs every dated figure the Institute publishes, and it is the one most easily broken by accident.

Every rate divides by the number of children of school age in a place. That number comes from the Census Bureau’s Population Estimates Program, and the Bureau issues a new vintage each year. A report describing a month uses the latest vintage that was public on the day that report would have been released, and never a later one.

A report published in February 2024 divides by Vintage 2022, because Vintage 2023 did not exist until 27 June 2024. Dividing by the newest estimates instead would describe a country nobody could see at the time, and would quietly restate a figure the Institute had already published. Both are the same error: a number whose date does not match the date on the page.

The release date recorded for each vintage is the Census server’s own timestamp for the file, which makes “was this public on that date” an answerable question rather than a recollection. Each extract is stored with its checksum, so a published figure cannot change underneath itself.

Sources: United States Census Bureau, Population Estimates Program, state characteristics files, and county characteristics files, ages five through seventeen, reference date 1 July of the vintage year.

Ranges

What the interval printed beside a rank is, and what it is not.

Where a report prints a range beside a state’s position, that range is a 90 per cent rank interval. It is computed, not estimated, and this is the computation.

An index is the mean of its constituents. The question a range answers is how much of a state’s position depends on which constituents happened to be in the set. To answer it, the constituent list is resampled with replacement to its own length, the fifty states are re-scored and re-ranked on that resample, and the state’s rank is recorded. That is repeated 2,000 times. The interval printed is the 5th to the 95th percentile of the 2,000 ranks — the band the state holds in ninety of every hundred resamples.

The draws are seeded, and seeded identically in every window. Two months’ intervals therefore differ only because the data differ, never because the sampler happened to run differently. A narrow band means the state’s position survives almost any redraw of the constituent set. A wide one means the position rests on a few constituents, and the reader is told so rather than left to assume otherwise.

What it does not include. The range measures sensitivity to which measures are counted. It does not measure source variability, it is not a confidence interval on a quantity in the world, and it carries no claim about how many children, schools, or enrollments exist. No source exists from which the second kind of uncertainty could honestly be built, so none is published, and the range is never described as though it were one.

Flagging

An unusual movement is marked, and then published unchanged.

A flag is a note to the reader. It is not a correction, it is not a suppression, and it never changes a figure.

The guard is arithmetic. For each state, the month-on-month change in each level index is compared against that state’s own median monthly change over the preceding sixty months. Where the month’s change exceeds three times that median on three or more of the level indices at once, the state is flagged for that window. The test is a state against its own five-year history. A small state and a large one are each held to their own record rather than to a common threshold.

What follows a flag. Nothing is withheld and nothing is smoothed. The figures publish exactly as computed, the flag is printed with them, and the report names the state and the window. A flag says that a movement is unusual against that state’s record and that a reader should treat one month of it as one month of it. It is not evidence that the measurement is wrong, and the Institute does not claim to know which it is.

What is never claimed

Three standing prohibitions.

Prohibition

No volume, and no growth

An index reading is a relative position. Nothing the Institute publishes describes a movement in an index as growth, decline, an increase, or a percentage of any quantity. Rank, rank movement, and the ratio to the median state are the three permitted forms.

Prohibition

No summation across indices

The Master Index and the four level indices are computed from disjoint constituent sets. The Master Index is not a total of the other four, and no page adds them together.

Prohibition

No attributed cause

Where the series moves sharply, the Institute reports the month and the size of the move and stops there. We do not attribute a cause to a movement we have not measured the cause of.

Before a document publishes

Every rule above is checked by a program, not by a reading.

Every figure in every document is recomputed from the published data before that document is released, and a document that fails any check is held until it is corrected. That applies to the figures themselves, to the sources credited beside them, to every chart drawn from them, to every map drawn from them, and to every claim stated in words. Nothing reaches a page on the strength of having been correct the last time it was written.

Correct use

Everything the Institute publishes is released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. You may copy it, redistribute it, and build on it for any purpose, including commercially, provided you meet the four conditions below.

Attribute
Credit the Spring Vale Christian Education Institute, name what you are citing, and link to it.
Cite the month with the data
Every index value belongs to the month it was measured in. A figure reproduced without its month is not our figure.
Never turn a position into a count
The indices measure relative position: where a state stands against the other forty-nine, and against its own record. They are not counts of children, schools, or enrollments, and a movement in an index is a change in standing rather than growth or decline in any quantity.
Mark what you changed
Adapted material must be identified as adapted, under section 3(a)(1)(B) of the license. Where an adaptation misstates what the indices measure, or presents altered figures as the Institute’s own, the Institute will exercise its right under section 3(a)(3) to require that attribution to the Institute be removed from it.
