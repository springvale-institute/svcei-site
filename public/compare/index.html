Compare

Put two states in the same frame and watch them trade places.

Put one state against another, against its Spring Vale Christian Education Institute Region, or against the median state, on one chart running from January 2004 to the current window. The view is written into the address, so a link reproduces exactly what you were looking at.

Where the ends of the table stand

The five states nearest the top and the two nearest the bottom, since 2004.

Before any two states are set against each other, here is the whole span they sit in. Eight lines is the ceiling of this form; a ninth begins to tangle, which is why the figure below this one takes two at a time.

Reading this chart

The standing view puts first in the country at the top and fiftieth at the bottom, so a line that sinks is a state losing ground to the other forty-nine. The ratio view puts each state against the median state for the same month: the dashed line is the median, and a state at two is oriented twice as strongly as the middle of the country.

A Region is drawn as the median of its member states, because relative positions do not add. A movement on either view is a change in standing rather than growth or decline in any quantity, and the Institute reports the movement and does not explain it, though we posit how and why it occurred in our research.

Correct use

Everything the Institute publishes is released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. You may copy it, redistribute it, and build on it for any purpose, including commercially, provided you meet the four conditions below.

Attribute
Credit the Spring Vale Christian Education Institute, name what you are citing, and link to it.
Cite the month with the data
Every index value belongs to the month it was measured in. A figure reproduced without its month is not our figure.
Never turn a position into a count
The indices measure relative position: where a state stands against the other forty-nine, and against its own record. They are not counts of children, schools, or enrollments, and a movement in an index is a change in standing rather than growth or decline in any quantity.
Mark what you changed
Adapted material must be identified as adapted, under section 3(a)(1)(B) of the license. Where an adaptation misstates what the indices measure, or presents altered figures as the Institute’s own, the Institute will exercise its right under section 3(a)(3) to require that attribution to the Institute be removed from it.
