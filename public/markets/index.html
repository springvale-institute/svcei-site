Christian Education Market Areas

The geography a schooling decision is actually made in.

A family does not necessarily choose among every Christian school in a state; families choose among the schools their children can be enrolled in. The Institute defines 262 Christian Education Market Areas, each a group of whole counties anchored on a population center, and quantifies the provision of Christian education inside them.

The map

All 262 market areas, shaded by Christian school enrollments per 1,000 children. Click any one.

Christian schooling, by level

Every jurisdiction as a column, mirrored by the level its schools serve.

A shaded map answers where. It has no axis, so it cannot answer how much, and the two questions have different answers. Each column here is one jurisdiction, ordered by its total: elementary and combined schools above the axis, secondary below. These are counts from the census and nothing on this figure may be divided into an index reading.

How far apart the market areas are

All 262, as dots, grouped into the eight Regions.

The maps above answer where the market areas are. A map has no axis, so it cannot answer how far apart they are, and that turns out to be the more interesting question: the spread inside a Region is wider than the spread between Regions. The vertical rule in each row is that Region’s median market area.

How thin the country is

Christian schooling is scarce across most of the map, and concentrated in a few places.

The map above shades market areas by their standing, which tells a reader who is ahead. This one shades them by the rate itself, which tells a reader how much schooling is actually there. The national rate is 60.0 Christian school enrollments per 1,000 children aged five through seventeen; the darkest areas run several times that, and much of the country runs well below it.

Where there is almost none

Market areas at or below 20 enrollments per 1,000 children.

One question, one answer. Twenty per thousand is one child in fifty, a threshold the Institute chose and states plainly rather than deriving from the data. The shaded areas are the places where Christian schooling exists as an exception rather than an option.

Time-lapse

Every month since January 2004, across all five indices.

Attention is measured for a state, so a map of attention alone shades every market area in one state the same and nothing inside a state ever moves. The first layer therefore sets each state’s attention standing against each market area’s own provision standing, which does move, market area by market area and month by month. The second layer is attention alone. Both carry all five indices.

Directory

Every market area, ranked by Christian school enrollments per 1,000 children.

Christian school enrollments per 1,000 children divides enrollments at Christian schools in the market area by the resident population aged five through seventeen there. It is a rate computed from two counts, and it is not an index reading. Filter by state, or type to find a market area.

Correct use

Everything the Institute publishes is released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. You may copy it, redistribute it, and build on it for any purpose, including commercially, provided you meet the four conditions below.

Attribute
Credit the Spring Vale Christian Education Institute, name what you are citing, and link to it.
Cite the month with the data
Every index value belongs to the month it was measured in. A figure reproduced without its month is not our figure.
Never turn a position into a count
The indices measure relative position: where a state stands against the other forty-nine, and against its own record. They are not counts of children, schools, or enrollments, and a movement in an index is a change in standing rather than growth or decline in any quantity.
Mark what you changed
Adapted material must be identified as adapted, under section 3(a)(1)(B) of the license. Where an adaptation misstates what the indices measure, or presents altered figures as the Institute’s own, the Institute will exercise its right under section 3(a)(3) to require that attribution to the Institute be removed from it.
