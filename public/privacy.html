The two maps, and what is behind them

This changed on 1 September 2026. From that date the Institute counts two things about how this site is used, and publishes both as maps. This section says exactly what is kept, because a map of readers is the kind of thing a reader is entitled to see described plainly.

Your ZIP code is not recorded. The table that turns five digits into a Christian Education Market Area is delivered with the page, so the lookup runs inside your own browser. The digits are not sent to the Institute and are not sent anywhere else. What comes back to us is which of the 262 market areas was reached, and the hour. A ZIP code is a street or two. A market area is a group of whole counties holding at least thirty thousand children of school age. We record the region and never the neighborhood.

Readership is counted by city, as a count. Our own server is told the approximate city a request came from, as every web server is. We keep a count against that city for that hour. We do not keep an address, a cookie, a session, a browser, a page, or a referring site alongside it.

Nothing here can identify you, by construction. The smallest thing either record can describe is one city in one hour, or one market area in one hour. Two visits by one person are indistinguishable from one visit by two, because there is nothing written down that could tell them apart. No identifier is created, so nothing can be linked to anything else you do here.

A browser sending Global Privacy Control is not counted at all. The page does not send the signal, and our server refuses it a second time on its own side. A rule enforced in one place is a rule that gets bypassed by accident.

No third party is involved. The counting, the storage, and the maps are ours. Nothing about a reader reaches anybody else.

The counts are published, on an unlisted page of this site, and they are never sold or shared. Write to privacy@springvale.institute if you would like the address.

What we cannot see, and will not claim to

A share is counted at the moment you click, and never afterward. If you send a page to WhatsApp, we record that somebody chose WhatsApp on that page. We do not learn who you sent it to, whether you sent it at all, whether anybody opened it, or what was said about it. No service can tell us those things, and we do not buy tools that claim to.

A citation is not tracked at all. When our work is quoted somewhere, nothing reports that to us. We find out the way anybody does — by being told, or by looking.

What we do not collect

  • We do not identify your employer or institution from your network address. Commercial services offer this. We have chosen not to use them.
  • We do not build cross-device or cross-site profiles. Google Signals is switched off in our analytics configuration.
  • We do not sell, rent, or share personal information for money or for targeted advertising, as those terms are defined in state privacy laws. There is no advertising on this site.
  • We do not use advertising cookies or advertising identifiers.
  • We do not require an account to read anything we publish.

Cookies and similar technologies

Analytics cookies are used to distinguish one visit from another. If you are reading from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, nothing that sets one is loaded until you have said yes to the choice this site offers you: saying nothing is a no. Everywhere else the standard the law sets is notice with an opt-out. This is that notice. Measurement runs, and you may switch it off at any moment.

Either way, the control is the same and it is on every page. Choose Your privacy choices in the footer to switch Google Analytics on or off. Your answer is remembered in your own browser and is sent nowhere.

Counting how many people read each page is done separately, by a service that sets no cookie, creates no identifier, and records nothing about you. That runs either way, and there is nothing in it to consent to.

We honor the Global Privacy Control. If your browser or an extension sends a GPC signal, we treat it as a valid opt-out of any sale or sharing and of targeted advertising, automatically, without you needing to contact us.

Children

This site is not directed to children under 13. It publishes research for school administrators, church leaders, journalists, policymakers, and researchers. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, write to privacy@springvale.institute and we will delete it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to know what personal information we hold about you, to obtain a copy, to correct it, to delete it, to opt out of any sale, sharing, or targeted advertising, and not to be treated differently for exercising any of these rights.

To exercise any right, write to privacy@springvale.institute. We will respond within 45 days. We do not charge for this. If we cannot verify who you are, we will say so rather than guess.

If you are in the EEA or the UK, you may also lodge a complaint with your supervisory authority.

Security and retention

Data is held only for the periods in the table above, and only in the systems named. Since we do not store network addresses or set advertising identifiers, there is very little to lose in the event of a breach. That is a deliberate design choice rather than an accident.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date above changes and the change is recorded on our corrections page alongside our other corrections. We do not change it quietly.
