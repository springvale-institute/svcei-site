The market area atlas

Two hundred and sixty-two market areas. Press play.

One map of every Christian Education Market Area in the country. Shade it by Attention or by Schools, and scrub it across the whole series. Hover any area for its figures; click it for its report.

What the three layers are

Attention is how strongly a geography is oriented toward Christian schooling, measured against the median state in the same month. It is measured for the fifty states. Schools is the census of Christian schools: how many exist, how large they are, and how many children are enrolled at them for every thousand school-age children in the area they serve. The third layer sets a market area’s own Schools standing against the attention standing of the state it sits in, and it is described further down.

Only the attention layer resolves into fifty shades

Attention is not measured for a market area, and none is estimated for one. On the attention layer alone, each market area carries the reading of the state it sits in, so the 262 areas resolve into fifty shades and every market area in one state is drawn the same. That is a limit of the measurement rather than a fault in the map, and it is why the tooltip on that layer names the state whose reading you are looking at. The District of Columbia has a Schools reading and has no Attention reading, so it is drawn unmeasured there.

The other two layers vary market area by market area, which is the reason this map exists. On the Schools layer, two market areas in the same state can differ by a factor of five in Christian school enrollments per 1,000 children, and no state map will ever show that. The third layer differs inside a state for the same reason: the state’s attention standing is held constant across it while each market area brings its own Schools standing, so what the shading varies with is the market area.

What the third layer is, and what it is not

It is a difference between two positions, each on its own 0 to 100 scale: the state’s standing on attention, and the market area’s own standing on Schools. Red is a market area whose state stands higher on attention than the market area stands on Schools; teal is the reverse.

It is not a quantity, it is not a rate, and it is not unmet demand. Neither measure is derived from the other, and a difference between two positions on two different denominators says that they disagree. It does not say that one produces the other, and the Institute makes no such claim.

Why the Schools layer does not move every month

The school census is taken rather than sampled monthly, so that layer steps when a new census lands while the attention layer moves every window. Moving the slider is still meaningful on both: it changes which census is in force and which population vintage the rate is computed against.

Correct use

Everything the Institute publishes is released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. You may copy it, redistribute it, and build on it for any purpose, including commercially, provided you meet the four conditions below.

Attribute
Credit the Spring Vale Christian Education Institute, name what you are citing, and link to it.
Cite the month with the data
Every index value belongs to the month it was measured in. A figure reproduced without its month is not our figure.
Never turn a position into a count
The indices measure relative position: where a state stands against the other forty-nine, and against its own record. They are not counts of children, schools, or enrollments, and a movement in an index is a change in standing rather than growth or decline in any quantity.
Mark what you changed
Adapted material must be identified as adapted, under section 3(a)(1)(B) of the license. Where an adaptation misstates what the indices measure, or presents altered figures as the Institute’s own, the Institute will exercise its right under section 3(a)(3) to require that attribution to the Institute be removed from it.
