The congregation atlas

Where the churches are. All 317,287 of them.

Where the churches are, how many people adhere to them, and how many Christian schools stand beside them. Two censuses joined on one geography.

Many churches, few schools

Schools per hundred congregations, market area by market area.

Two censuses on one geography. The darkest places have the most Christian schools standing beside a hundred Christian congregations; the palest have the fewest. It is a count divided by a count. It is not a target, it is not a claim that a church ought to run a school, and it says nothing about why any place sits where it does.

Why this is a county map and not a map of pins

The 2020 U.S. Religion Census is the only census of American congregations: 372 religious bodies and 356,642 congregations. It is county level by policy rather than by accident. Asked whether it provides church addresses, its own documentation answers “Absolutely not.” Names and addresses are never collected and never published.

That suits this geography exactly. A Christian Education Market Area is a group of whole counties, so every congregation in the file lands in exactly one market area and the totals reconcile to the digit. There is no geocoding, no address matching, and no coverage question.

The Institute could assemble a map of pins from the sources that do carry addresses. It would hold a fraction of these congregations, and it would miss small independent churches worst of all — which is the opposite of what a study of Christian schooling needs. A map that claims to show every church while holding two thirds of them is the kind of claim this Institute exists to avoid.

Which bodies are counted

A body is counted as Christian where it professes Jesus Christ and holds scripture that includes the New Testament. That rule is deliberately wider than any one tradition’s judgment of who is orthodox: the Institute is describing a religious landscape, not certifying doctrine. Three bodies are contested in three different directions — the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, Jehovah’s Witnesses, and the Unitarian Universalist Association — and together they are 7.8% of all congregations in the file. Each is excluded from these figures, counted separately, and flagged for a recorded ruling rather than settled quietly. Every one of the 372 bodies and its classification is published.

Schools per hundred congregations

Two censuses on one geography: Christian schools from the federal private school census, Christian congregations from the Religion Census. Their ratio says how many schools stand beside a hundred churches. It is a count divided by a count, it is not a causal claim, it is not a target, and it says nothing about why any place sits where it does.

Correct use

Everything the Institute publishes is released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. You may copy it, redistribute it, and build on it for any purpose, including commercially, provided you meet the four conditions below.

Attribute
Credit the Spring Vale Christian Education Institute, name what you are citing, and link to it.
Cite the month with the data
Every index value belongs to the month it was measured in. A figure reproduced without its month is not our figure.
Never turn a position into a count
The indices measure relative position: where a state stands against the other forty-nine, and against its own record. They are not counts of children, schools, or enrollments, and a movement in an index is a change in standing rather than growth or decline in any quantity.
Mark what you changed
Adapted material must be identified as adapted, under section 3(a)(1)(B) of the license. Where an adaptation misstates what the indices measure, or presents altered figures as the Institute’s own, the Institute will exercise its right under section 3(a)(3) to require that attribution to the Institute be removed from it.
